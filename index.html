The pledge

We are not against transparency. We are against a yes/no watermark that treats a coriander spelling fix the same as a fully generated article.

Article 50 of the EU AI Act is supposed to make people aware of AI-generated content. Recital 133 already draws the line: machine-readable marking is not required where AI is only helping with basic editing, or has not substantially changed the meaning of the original content. Blanket-marking everything is not compliance. It is a liability hedge, paid for by the user.

An invisible mark does not inform a reader. It informs a detector. That is not the transparency the law describes.

What we believe

  1. Assisted is not generated.
  2. The human who brings the idea, steers the work, and chooses what to keep is the author. The model is a tool, closer to a colleague than a rights-holder.
  3. A provenance record may say a model touched a file. It must not be allowed to read as ownership of the user’s judgement.
  4. Customers should not have to leave the product, or go around it, to keep their own intellectual creation.
  5. Fully generated slop should be marked. Proofreading, grammar, translation and light edit should not wear the same stamp.

What we ask of AI labs

Anthropic, and every signatory of the EU Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content, including OpenAI, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral and Cohere.

  1. Respect Recital 133. Stop blanket-watermarking. Do not treat “the model touched this” as “the model generated this.”
  2. Mark by intent. If the user asked to proofread, do not apply a generated-content watermark. If the user asked to create, mark it. You already have the prompt.
  3. Use provenance states, not a binary stamp: human-origin, lightly edited, translated, transformed, fully generated. Show signer, model, version, confidence, and a way to contest the label.
  4. Make credit visible on fully generated work: Ideated by [user], processed by [model].
  5. Do not degrade the output to hide a signal. Do not shift tokens away from the best answer, and do not apply synonym-style marks that can break code.
  6. Publish the rules. What is marked, on which models, from which date, and how a user challenges a mark on their own text.

What we, the signatories, commit to

  • We will be honest when a model wrote the piece.
  • We will not pretend that directing, editing and selecting output is “doing nothing.” That selection is the work.
  • We will not accept a secret, unreadable stamp as a substitute for transparency.
  • We will take this to the labs we pay, and to the ones that have not yet shipped the same mark.

Open letter

From: Sara Simeone

To: Anthropic, and Code of Practice signatories including OpenAI, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral, and Cohere

Re: Machine-readable marking under Article 50(2) and Recital 133

I write as a paying customer, and as someone whose post on this subject has now been read and argued over in public.

The reaction was not “users hate transparency.” It was more precise than that. People will accept a mark when a model did the work. They will not accept a silent stamp on text they originated, directed, and only asked you to tidy.

You have signed a Code of Practice that is supposed to help you comply with Article 50 of the EU AI Act. Recital 133 is not ambiguous. Machine-readable marking is not required where the AI is only helping with basic editing, or has not substantially changed the meaning of the original content. Blanket-watermarking everything is not a faithful reading of that rule. It is the cheapest way to avoid defending “substantially changed” case by case.

An invisible watermark also fails the stated goal of the law. Article 50 is about making people aware of AI-generated content. A mark no reader can see does not inform a human. It informs a detector, later, on someone else’s terms. That is provenance for platforms, not transparency for users.

Your own documentation already admits the problem. A Claude mark can appear on text the user wrote, then asked you to proofread, translate, or convert. If the signal fires on a coriander spelling fix, it is not proving authorship. It is corrupting the record.

Paying customers should not have to leave your product, paste into a Google Doc, or invent workarounds to keep ownership of their own thinking. If you ban people for going around a mark you applied too broadly, you are punishing the human in the loop for a design choice you made.

I am not asking you to ignore the law. I am asking you to comply with it properly.
